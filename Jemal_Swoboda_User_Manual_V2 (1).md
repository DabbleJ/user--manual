# User Guide: Jemal Swoboda
## How to Work with Me Effectively

**V2 draft, October 2026**

---

## Introduction

This is a working agreement, not a personality profile. It describes how I actually work, including where I slip, so you can get my best thinking faster.

Where I am still building a habit, I say so with **(working on)**. Where I do it reliably, I state it flat.

---

## I. How I Define Success

Success is trust and leverage: you can hand me work and know it will be right, on time, and one step ahead.

**What you would see by day 30:**
- Work that needs light edits
- Status updates that arrive on schedule without being asked
- Me confirming whether something is a build or an idea before I run with it
- A same-day question ("is this intentional or overlooked?") when something looks off, so I neither sit on a real risk nor raise a false alarm

The benchmark I hold myself to: at a past job, my boss was comfortable sending me to meetings in his place because I understood the system and the rules and he did not have to wonder if I would go off script. I would like that trust in 30 days.

**Before I call anything client-ready, I ask:**
- Did we answer the question?
- Does it solve the problem?
- Does it add more value than was asked for?
- What move two or three steps ahead can we address now?
- Beyond that: no typos, clean formatting, no AI filler.

---

## II. How I Think and Make Decisions

- **I build first and ask second.** I turn an idea into a rough working version in 10 to 15 minutes, put it in front of someone, and refine. I used to talk ideas around until I had enough encouragement and then never build them. The test now is whether it works and whether someone uses it. My D.O.N.E. family task app is an example: my wife and I use it, and I have changed it based on that use.
- **A test changes my mind faster than an argument.** In Dabble's early expansion I believed we needed confirmed event partners before entering a market. An investor suggested cloning proven events into new markets and finding supply after demand appeared. I tested it, I was wrong, and it became how we grew.
- **I stop ideating when something exists that people can react to,** not when the idea feels finished.
- **I have used a time box to force a decision.** When Brit + Co's affiliate revenue fell, I asked for two days to produce a realistic plan to move content to our own platform, with the rule that if the data was not moved by the end of the week we would not proceed. I tested the workflow with our lead affiliate editor first, who built a story noticeably faster, and that gave me the confidence to proceed.
- **When I'm rushed, I skip two checks (working on):**
  1. Is this solving the real problem, or routing around friction a quicker fix would resolve?
  2. Does the person who owns the work agree with the direction?

  If you want to be asked first, say so up front.

## III. How to Work with Me

**Bring me:**
- The outcome or change you want
- A proposal for how we reach alignment
- One line on how it landed, if that matters. I take it seriously.

**Leave out:**
- A long backstory or reconstruction of the cause
- Blame
- A problem with no proposed shift

**When we are both thinking out loud:**
- I'll lean on "directive or idea?"
- One of us has to say "park it" or "build it"
- Let's rank the ideas we want to pursue, so building isn't driven by whoever spoke last
- If I'm ideating and rambling, ask me for a yes or no
- If I'm building ahead of the plan, tell me to stop and align

**When we disagree:** I would rather propose a quick test than argue. I will bring my reasoning and a way to check it, and I will change my mind if the test says I am wrong. If I think you are missing something, I will say so directly.

**How I lead a conversation:** If I have conviction and you need direction out of ambiguity, I lead with the point. If I want your perspective and your ownership, I may wander until we have alignment and then land the point. Sometimes I ramble without meaning to. If I'm circling and you're lost, ask me what the point is.

## IV. Where I Add Value

**Highest value:**
- Open brainstorming
- Turning an idea into a working prototype fast
- Testing a hypothesis cheaply before a team commits
- Doing the one thing that is needed once the decision is made

**Lowest value:**
- Social posts and marketing promotion. I can do them well, but they are a poor use of my time, and when I do work I dread, I move quickly without effectiveness or joy.

**First thing I would improve about a team's systems:** reduce the clicks, windows, and folder levels it takes to find and use things, with flatter directories and an AI assistant connected to our files. Example: at B-Line, I took a Google Drive for new growth markets that had one folder with 8 sub-folders and many untagged loose files and reduced it to 3 folders with no orphaned files, indexed and tagged for search.

## V. What Frustrates Me and Why

These are friction signals, not complaints about people.

| Signal | Because | What helps |
|---|---|---|
| Asking more than once for something we agreed would be done | It tells me my time or the work isn't valued, and I remember it the next time I ask | Tell me before the deadline passes if it will slip, with a new date |
| AI output presented as finished work that I still have to improve | It looks like effort without judgment, and the fixing lands on me | Show what you checked and changed, not only what the tool produced |
| A slow response with no heads-up | I can't tell whether it is being handled or whether the work matters to the person | Agree on response times at the start and flag when you can't meet them |

I have done the second one myself and have shared AI drafts before fully polishing them **(working on)**.

## VI. Communication Standards

- **Calls are my favorite channel for honesty.** If I go quiet, which is rare, call me and I will tell you why. When a call is more noise than needed, I follow with a short written recap.
- **Response times:** within about an hour on Slack during my working hours, by the start of my next working block otherwise, and within a day on email.
- **Schedule:** about 20 hours a week, flexible to overlap with Michael, starting after 9 AM Pacific.
- **Status updates:** by day 30, you will get them on a schedule without having to ask.
- **How to raise a risk with me:** be direct. A phone call is best, or Slack or text. If a risk needs to be raised and addressed, it usually means I dropped the ball on communicating my progress or plan, so I want to hear it plainly.
- **Urgent items outside my working hours** need to meet criteria we agree on up front. This is a 20-hour role and I like boundaries. I enjoy getting things done and fixing things, so I watch for scope and hours creep. If you see it, tell me.
- **If a deadline looks at risk,** it usually means I have not shown my progress clearly. I rarely miss deadlines, but I can postpone work when I am confident and then miss a small detail. Example: I once had to email millions of Snapguide users before a domain expired. I tested the plan, missed our daily send cap, and had to upgrade our plan quickly to finish in time. I am working on checking constraints like send limits before I start.

## VII. Feedback and Growth

**Receiving.** My first reaction to hard feedback is to feel defeated, my second is to get defensive. Within a week I turn it into something I can work on. A former boss once told me a role I was considering was likely not a good fit, and I spent the next week working on how I present myself.

**Giving.** I used to wait until a mistake repeated because I didn't want to be the bad guy. Now I raise missed expectations right away **(working on)**. I ask for a quick call, name the issue before anyone reads into it, and ask how I can help and what needs to be rebalanced. If the pattern continues, I ask the person to compare their work against their role and tell me where the gaps are, then agree on a plan and follow up. I do not write off a missed expectation as a one-time mistake. I remember it, which is one reason I address it early.

## VIII. Standards

**I won't compromise on:** presenting anything as verified that isn't confirmed. Client-facing work is either checked or clearly flagged as uncertain.

**I'm flexible on:** how early I share drafts and how polished they are. I default to shipping early and often internally, and I'll adjust to how you work.

**My editing pass:** I run a saved prompt, built from my past mistakes, that checks for commonly overlooked items and the next two or three moves ahead. I never send an AI-supported draft without my own editorial review.

## IX. What Energizes Me and What I Need

**Energizes me:**
- Building something real for a specific person. The last stretch where I lost track of time was building a platform for an NGO in Tanzania that digitizes annual school exams done on paper.
- Team projects.
- A week where nothing carries over to the next Monday, because I was realistic about what I took on and automated what repeats **(working on)**.

**What I need:**
- **Acknowledgement.** I tell myself I don't need it, but hearing it motivates me. A verbal call-out in front of another team lands best, especially when my work has added value for people outside my own team.
- **Early alignment on timing and ownership.**
- **To be pulled in.** When I'm new and want to prove myself, I take problems on alone. In 2021 I was asked to fix an AWS setup that was running up a large bill, and worked on it by myself. It would have been better to lean on the team. I'd like to be asked to pair **(working on)**.

## X. Honest Truths

- **People assume I'm a lone wolf.** I'm efficient and I run lean teams, but I love working as part of a team. Sometimes the misread is partly my doing.
- **Two things that are both true:** I'm good at open brainstorming and good at doing the one thing that is needed.
- **A quirk:** I build first and ask second, so you may see a working version before you were asked.
- **A phrase you'll hear:** "How can we do this more efficiently, or do you have another way to solve it?"
- **A habit with a cost:** I form friendships. As an early founder and leader at Dabble, I hired fast after a funding round and then waited too long to act when someone wasn't a fit, which hurt the teammates who were performing. I have learned a lot since then, and I now address missed expectations right away.
- **What would make me leave a working relationship:** not being challenged or used well after I've asked for an adjustment, not being given the respect I've asked for, or being asked to do something that isn't aligned with my values.
