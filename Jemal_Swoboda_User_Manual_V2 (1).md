# How to Work with Jemal Swoboda
## My Working Manual

**V2.3 draft, October 2026**

---

## Introduction

This is my guide for us working well together and I hope it reads as an invitation, not a rulebook. It describes how I actually work, including where I slip, so you can get my best thinking faster. Where I'm still building a habit, I say so with **(working on)**. Where I do it reliably, I state it flat. I'd like to learn how you work too.

---

## I. What Success Looks Like

Success is trust and leverage: you can hand me work and know it will be right, on time, and one step ahead.

**What you'd see by day 30:**
- Work that needs light edits
- Status updates that arrive on schedule without being asked
- Me confirming whether something is a build or an idea before I run with it
- A same-day question ("is this intentional or overlooked?") when something looks off

The benchmark I hold myself to: at a past job, my boss was comfortable sending me to meetings in his place because I understood the system and the rules and he didn't have to wonder if I'd go off script. I'd like that trust in 30 days.

**Before I call anything client-ready, I ask:**
- Did we answer the question?
- Does it solve the problem?
- Does it add more value than was asked for?
- What move two or three steps ahead can we address now?

---

## II. How I Decide

- **I align first, then build fast (working on).** I want to confirm the real problem and that the person who owns the work agrees with the direction. Then I turn the idea into a rough working version quickly, with AI tools it can take minutes, and refine it with real feedback. I once shipped a feature at Brit + Co before checking with the person who owned the experience, and it cost us a follow-up meeting a quick message would have avoided.
- **A test changes my mind faster than an argument.** In Dabble's early expansion I believed we needed confirmed event partners before entering a market. An investor suggested starting with proven events and finding supply after demand appeared. I tested it, I was wrong, and it became how we grew.
- **I stop ideating when something exists that people can react to,** not when the idea feels finished.
- **Under time pressure,** I have a tendency to skip answering the following (working on): Does this solve the real problem, and does the person who owns it agree with the direction? A quick “which problem does this solve, and who owns it?” brings me back to aligning first.

## III. How to Work with Me

**Bring me:**
- The outcome or change you want
- A proposal for how we reach alignment
- One line on how it landed, if that matters. I take it seriously.

**Leave out:** a long backstory, blame, or a problem with no proposed shift.

**When we're both thinking out loud:**
- I'll lean on "directive or idea?"
- One of us has to say "park it" or "build it"
- Let's rank the ideas we want to pursue, so building isn't driven by whoever spoke last
- If I'm ideating and rambling, ask me for a yes or no

**When we disagree:** I'd rather propose a quick test than argue. I'll bring my reasoning and a way to check it, and I'll change my mind if the test says I'm wrong. If I think you're missing something, I'll say so directly.

**How I lead a conversation:** If you need direction out of ambiguity, I lead with the point. If I want your perspective and ownership, I may wander until we have alignment and then land the point. If I'm circling and you're lost, ask me what the point is.

## IV. Where I Add Value

**My value is highest in:**
- Open brainstorming, especially when the team needs to own the answer
- Turning an idea into a working prototype fast, so people react to something real
- Testing a hypothesis cheaply before a team commits, and agreeing up front what would change our minds
- Fixing what's broken across teams, especially problems with no obvious owner
- Marketplace supply and partner economics: why people join, stay, and pay
- Simplifying systems so people spend less time hunting and clicking
- Using AI tools to get from a first draft to something executive-ready faster

**My value is lowest in:**
- Social posts and marketing promotion. I can do them well, but they're a poor use of my time, and I move quickly through them without effectiveness or joy.
- Pure spreadsheet and numbers work with no decision attached
- Doing the same manual step every week. I'll automate it or hand it off.

**If you want to support me well:**
- Give me the outcome you want, and ask me for a yes or no when I'm ideating
- Help me choose what to build next and when to stop. I generate a lot of ideas, so I do best with shared priorities and a definition of "done."
- Tell me early when something needs a second look, so a small detail doesn't slip
- Tell me where my work added value for you or for other teams

**First thing I'd improve about a team's systems:** the number of clicks, windows, and folder levels it takes to find things. At B-Line, I turned a sprawling shared drive for new growth markets into a small set of tagged, searchable folders.

## V. What Drains Me and Why

Each of these points to something in how we work that we can fix, not to a person to blame.

| Signal | Because | What helps |
|---|---|---|
| Me asking more than once for something we agreed would be done | It tells me my time or the work isn't valued, and I remember it the next time I ask | Tell me before the deadline passes if it will slip, with a new date |
| AI output presented as finished work that I still have to improve | It looks like effort without judgment, and the fixing lands on me | Show what you checked and changed, not only what the tool produced |
| A slow response with no heads-up | I can't tell whether it's being handled or whether the work matters to the person | Agree on response times at the start and flag when you can't meet them |

## VI. Time and Communication

- **Hours:** 20 hours a week, flexible to overlap with Michael.
- **Response times:** I'm usually quick on Slack and email during my working hours and respond to email within a day after hours. Let's agree on expectations up front.
- **After hours:** I'll adapt to the team's needs and I'm open to negotiating after-hours work, with expectations agreed up front.
- **Calls are my favorite channel for honesty.** If I go quiet, which is rare, call me and I'll tell you why. When a call is more noise than needed, I follow with a short written recap.
- **Deadlines and risks:** if a deadline looks at risk, it usually means I haven't shown my progress clearly. Tell me directly, by phone if possible, or Slack or text. I rarely miss deadlines, but I can postpone work when I'm confident and then miss a detail. I once missed a daily send cap on a large Snapguide email and had to upgrade our plan quickly to finish in time. I'm working on checking constraints before I start **(working on)**.

## VII. Feedback and Growth

**Receiving.** Direct feedback helps me, and I’d like it early and plainly. I try to approach with curiosity and listen first, ask a question or two so I understand it, and work not to defend. I work to pick a specific change and follow up to confirm it landed. When a former boss told me a role wasn’t a good fit, I took it seriously and made a plan: I worked on developing the skills she felt I  lacked and on how I present myself.

**Giving.** I raise missed expectations right away **(working on)**. I ask for a quick call, name the issue before anyone reads into it, and ask how I can help and what needs to be rebalanced. If the pattern continues, I ask the person to compare their work against their role and tell me where the gaps are, then agree on a plan and follow up.

## VIII. Standards

**I won't compromise on:** presenting anything as verified that isn't confirmed. Client-facing work is either checked or clearly flagged as uncertain.

**I'm flexible on:** how early I share drafts and how polished they are. I share early internally and I'll adjust to how you work.

**My editing pass:** I run a saved prompt, built from my past mistakes, that checks for commonly overlooked items and the next two or three moves ahead. I never send an AI-supported draft without my own editorial review.

**Energizes me:**
- Building something real that matters, like the platform I built for Rift Valley Children's Village in Tanzania, that digitizes annual school wellness exams previously done on paper improving local staff time and focus making kids healthy
- Team projects where everyone gets to work in their sweet spot, the overlap of what they’re good at and what they love
- Outside work: running, baking, live music, and raising my curious kids in the PNW with my rockstar trauma therapist wife
- A week where nothing carries over to the next Monday **(working on)**

**What I need:**
- **Acknowledgement.** Hearing it motivates me, even though I tell myself I don't need it. A verbal call-out in front of another team lands best, especially when my work has added value for people outside my own team.
- **Early alignment on timing and ownership.**
- **To be pulled in.** When I'm new and want to prove myself, I take problems on alone. In 2021 I worked on an AWS cost problem at Brit + Co by myself when it would have been better to lean on the team. I'd like to be asked to pair **(working on)**.

- **People assume I'm a lone wolf.** I'm efficient and I run lean teams, but I love working as part of a team. Sometimes the misread is partly my doing.
- **Two things that are both true:** I'm good at open brainstorming and good at doing the one thing that's needed.
- **Where I'm headed:** I want to scale this role into a full-time partnership. If that isn't in the cards, look to me exploring other roles to support my financial and career goals. Until then, I want to learn, be mentored, and build my skills and leadership confidence.
- **A phrase you'll hear:** "How can we do this more efficiently?"
- **A habit with a cost:** I form friendships. As an early founder and leader at Dabble, I hired fast after a funding round and then waited too long to act when someone wasn't a fit, which hurt the teammates who were performing. I've learned a lot since then.
- **What would make me leave a working relationship:** not being challenged or used well after I've asked for an adjustment, not being given the respect I've asked for, or being asked to do something that isn't aligned with my values.
